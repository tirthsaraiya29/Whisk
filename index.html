On context and the shape of thought

#thinking#notes

A note is only as useful as the context around it. The best ideas don't arrive in isolation — they grow from the friction between things you've already written, read, and half-remembered.

The problem with folders

Most tools ask you to decide where a thought belongs before you've finished thinking it. Whisk lets notes link to each other, so the structure emerges from the work itself.

What changes

When your editor knows what's on the page, what's in the graph, and what you've been working on — the assistance stops being generic

Philosophy

Built around a few
quiet convictions.

Whisk isn't trying to be everything. It's built around a small set of beliefs about how people actually think, write, and work — and what gets in the way.

01

Notes should stay connected.

Ideas don't live in folders. Whisk treats every note as a node in a larger map — linked, referenced, and discoverable through the work itself.

02

AI should read the room.

Generic assistants answer generic questions. Whisk's AI sees the note you're writing, the graph around it, and the workspace you're in — so its help is actually relevant.

03

Your data stays yours.

Local-first by default. Your notes live on your machine, your models run where you point them, and nothing about your workspace has to touch a server you don't control.

Capabilities

A workspace that earns
the things it promises.

Every feature below is something Whisk actually does today. No roadmaps dressed up as shipping, no integrations that live in a slide deck.

01 — Writing

Markdown, rendered the way you'd write it.

Whisk treats markdown as a first-class surface, not an import format. Headings, links, code, tags — all of it renders cleanly, edits quickly, and stays portable.

  • Live-rendered markdown with familiar shortcuts
  • Plain files on disk — open them anywhere
  • Tags, links, and structure that survive export

02 — Connections

A knowledge graph that grows with the writing.

Every link you make, every tag you add, every note you reference — Whisk weaves it into a graph you can actually navigate. Not a novelty view. A working map of what you know.

  • Interactive graph of notes, links, and tags
  • Backlinks surfaced where they matter
  • Structure that emerges instead of being imposed
03 — Intelligence

AI that works inside the workspace.

Whisk's assistant doesn't open in a separate tab. It lives next to your notes, reads the document you're in, and can reach into the graph around it. Help that's actually in context, not pasted into a chat window.

  • Context-aware responses drawn from your notes
  • Summaries, rewrites, and continuations in place
  • Works with local models or the provider you choose

04 — Retrieval

Find things fast, without leaving the keyboard.

Whisk indexes your workspace locally. A single shortcut pulls up a command-style search that jumps you to notes, links, and references — with snippets that show you why the result matched.

  • Instant full-text search across the workspace
  • Keyboard-first, command-palette interaction
  • Results ranked by relevance, not recency alone

The AI workspace

An assistant that knows what you're working on.

Whisk doesn't bolt a chatbot onto a notes app. It builds the assistant out of the workspace itself — the note on screen, the links around it, the models you've chosen.

Most AI assistants start from a blank prompt. Whisk starts from your workspace — the document, its neighbours, the tags, the models you've picked — and builds the conversation on top.

Context, assembled for you

Whisk gathers the relevant notes, links, and tags automatically — so you can ask about the work instead of copying it into a chat.

Local models, your choice

Point Whisk at the model you trust. Run it on your machine, on your server, or through the provider you prefer. The workspace doesn't care.

Tools, not just text

Through MCP, the assistant can reach into your workspace as a tool — editing notes, walking the graph, and acting on what it finds.

Control

Your workspace, your machine,
your rules.

Whisk is local-first because that's the simplest way to give you real control. No account to create, no data to migrate out later, no telemetry to audit.

Notes live on your disk

Plain markdown files in a folder you choose. Open them with any editor, back them up with any tool, version them however you like.

Models you pick

Run a local model, point at a self-hosted endpoint, or use a provider. Whisk doesn't decide for you — and it doesn't phone home either way.

No account required

Download, open, start writing. There is no sign-up step, no cloud tier to upsell, and no moment where your workspace stops working without a subscription.

Portable by design

Because Whisk stores your work as regular files, leaving is trivial. There's no export wizard because there's nothing to export — it's already your data.

A quieter kind of workspace.

Whisk is open source, local-first, and built for people who want their tools to stay out of the way. If that sounds like the room you've been looking for — come take a seat.
